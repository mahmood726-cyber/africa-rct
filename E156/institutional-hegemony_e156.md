# INSTITUTIONAL HEGEMONY

Does the geographic clustering of clinical trials in Africa reflect commercial extraction or institutional centralization? This forensic audit applied Benford's Law analysis to the location counts of 6,684 interventional trials across Africa and Europe (Germany) using trial-level data from ClinicalTrials.gov. The Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD) for location counts in Africa was 0.0675, indicating "Critical Non-Conformity" and extreme structural clustering compared to the theoretical Benford distribution. Segmented analysis by sponsor class revealed that Academic and NIH-funded trials exhibited significantly higher geographic non-conformity (MAD: 0.0778) than Industry-sponsored trials (MAD: 0.0262). These results suggest that "Institutional Hegemony"—the concentration of research within a few elite academic hubs—is the primary driver of geographic research deserts in Africa, surpassing commercial interests in its degree of centralization. While enrollment counts in both regions conformed to Benford's Law (Africa MAD: 0.0086), the site distribution data points to a systemic exclusion of secondary and rural centers from the academic research infrastructure.
